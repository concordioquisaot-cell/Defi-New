How Money Legos Work

Imagine building a financial application where you can:

  1. Take a loan from Compound (lending protocol)
  2. Swap the borrowed asset on Uniswap (DEX)
  3. Deposit the swapped asset into Yearn Finance (yield aggregator) to earn yield
  4. Use the yield-bearing token as collateral to mint a stablecoin on MakerDAO
  5. Use that stablecoin to provide liquidity on Curve Finance

All of this happens programmatically without human intervention, enabled by smart contracts that can call each other directly.

This composability creates a vibrant ecosystem where innovation happens at an unprecedented pace. However, it also introduces new risks and complexities that we'll explore throughout this level.

DAI: The DeFi Native Stablecoin

DAI, created by MakerDAO, is the most prominent crypto-collateralized stablecoin. Here's how it works:

1. Deposit Collateral

User deposits ETH into a Maker Vault as collateral (typically 150%+ of the DAI to be minted).

2. Generate DAI

Smart contract mints DAI against the collateral, creating a debt position that must be repaid with interest (stability fee).

3. Maintain Position

If ETH price drops significantly, the position may be liquidated to protect the DAI peg. Users can add collateral or repay DAI to avoid liquidation.

Stablecoins are essential for DeFi because they allow users to hold value without exposure to crypto volatility while still being able to participate in yield-generating activities. They serve as the primary trading pairs on DEXs, collateral in lending protocols, and the base currency for many DeFi applications.

Decentralized Exchanges (DEXs)

Decentralized Exchanges allow users to trade cryptocurrencies directly from their wallets without intermediaries. Unlike centralized exchanges (CEXs) like Coinbase or Binance, DEXs don't hold user funds - trades occur peer-to-contract via smart contracts.

Types of DEXs

AMM DEXs

Use liquidity pools and mathematical formulas to determine prices. No order books needed.

Examples: Uniswap, Sushiswap, PancakeSwap
Order Book DEXs

Traditional order book model but executed on-chain. More familiar but less efficient on-chain.

Examples: dYdX, Serum (Solana)
Aggregator DEXs

Route trades across multiple DEXs to find the best prices and minimize slippage.

Examples: 1inch, Matcha, Paraswap

DEXs represent a fundamental shift in how trading occurs. By eliminating intermediaries, they reduce counterparty risk, increase transparency, and provide global access to financial markets. The AMM model, while innovative, introduces new concepts like liquidity provision, impermanent loss, and slippage that users must understand.

Lending & Borrowing Protocols

DeFi lending platforms allow users to lend their crypto assets to earn interest or borrow assets by providing collateral. Unlike traditional banks, these protocols operate algorithmically with smart contracts determining interest rates based on supply and demand.

Lenders

Deposit assets to earn passive income. Interest accrues in real-time and compounds automatically.

Benefits:
  • Higher yields than traditional savings
  • 24/7 access to funds (no lock-up periods)
  • Transparent, algorithmic interest rates
  • No credit checks or KYC requirements
APY: 3-8%

Borrowers

Use crypto holdings as collateral to borrow other assets. Maintain loans with over-collateralization.

Use Cases:
  • Access liquidity without selling assets
  • Leverage trading positions
  • Tax-efficient strategies
  • Yield farming with borrowed assets
APR: 5-15%

How Lending Protocols Work: AAVE Example

1
Deposit

User deposits ETH into AAVE's liquidity pool, receiving aTokens that represent their deposit and accrue interest.

2
Borrow

Another user borrows DAI against their ETH collateral, paying interest that goes to liquidity providers.

3
Health Factor

The protocol monitors each borrower's collateral ratio. If it drops below threshold, positions can be liquidated.

4
Withdraw

Lenders can withdraw their deposit + interest at any time by redeeming their aTokens for the underlying asset.

Key Metrics to Understand

LTV (Loan-to-Value)

The maximum you can borrow against your collateral (e.g., 75% LTV means you can borrow up to 75% of your collateral's value).

Health Factor

A number representing the safety of your loan. If it drops below 1, your position may be liquidated.

Liquidation Threshold

The collateral value at which your position becomes eligible for liquidation (usually higher than LTV).

Utilization Rate

The percentage of deposited assets that are currently borrowed. Affects interest rates.

DeFi lending protocols democratize access to credit while providing attractive yields to lenders. However, they require careful risk management, particularly regarding collateral ratios and potential liquidations during market volatility.

Understanding Impermanent Loss

Impermanent loss occurs when the price ratio of tokens in a liquidity pool changes compared to when you deposited them. The larger the change, the greater the loss relative to simply holding the tokens.

Scenario: ETH/DAI Pool

You deposit 1 ETH ($2,000) and 2,000 DAI when ETH price is $2,000.

If ETH price doubles to $4,000, arbitrageurs will adjust the pool, leaving you with approximately 0.707 ETH and 2,828 DAI.

Result: You'd have $5,656 total vs. $6,000 if you just held (5.7% impermanent loss).

When It Becomes Permanent

The loss is "impermanent" because if prices return to their original ratio when you withdraw, the loss disappears.

It becomes permanent when you withdraw at a different price ratio than when you deposited.

Key Insight: High trading fees can compensate for impermanent loss in active pools.

Yield Farming Strategies

Basic Farming

Provide liquidity to a single pool and earn trading fees + liquidity mining rewards.

Leveraged Farming

Borrow additional assets to increase position size, amplifying both gains and risks.

Yield Aggregation

Use protocols like Yearn Finance that automatically move funds between strategies to optimize returns.

LP Token Farming

Take LP tokens from one pool and stake them in another protocol to earn additional rewards.

Yield farming represents the frontier of DeFi innovation, offering potentially high returns but with corresponding risks. Successful yield farmers must understand not just APY percentages but also the underlying mechanics, risks, and how to manage impermanent loss.

Understanding DeFi Risks

While DeFi offers unprecedented opportunities, it also comes with significant risks. Understanding these risks is essential for safe participation in decentralized finance.

Smart Contract Risk

Bugs or vulnerabilities in smart contract code can lead to loss of funds. Even audited contracts can have undiscovered issues.

Mitigation: Use well-audited protocols, start with small amounts, diversify across platforms

Impermanent Loss

Providing liquidity exposes you to potential losses when token prices diverge from their initial ratio.

Mitigation: Choose stablecoin pairs, understand the math, ensure fees compensate for risk

Oracle Risk

DeFi protocols rely on external price feeds (oracles). Manipulated or incorrect data can cause liquidations or incorrect trades.

Mitigation: Use protocols with decentralized oracles, understand their price feed mechanisms

Additional Key Risks

Liquidation Risk

Borrowed positions can be liquidated if collateral value drops or borrowed asset value increases significantly.

Example: Borrowing against ETH during a market crash
Governance Risk

Protocol changes are voted on by token holders. Majority holders or coordinated groups can push through changes that may not benefit all users.

Example: Changing fee structures or reward distributions
Regulatory Risk

Evolving regulations could impact DeFi protocols, potentially limiting access or changing how they operate.

Example: KYC requirements for DeFi interactions

Risk Management Best Practices

Start Small

Never invest more than you can afford to lose. Start with small amounts to learn the platforms.

Diversify

Spread investments across different protocols, chains, and asset types to reduce systemic risk.

Do Your Own Research

Understand each protocol's mechanics, audit history, team, and community before investing.

Monitor Positions

Regularly check your positions, especially during market volatility, to avoid liquidations.

DeFi's permissionless nature means you are your own bank and risk manager. While the potential rewards are significant, they come with responsibility. The most successful DeFi participants are those who thoroughly understand both the opportunities and the risks.
